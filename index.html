What Is a Japanese Car Auction Sheet?

A Japanese car auction sheet is an inspection report prepared when a used vehicle is listed for sale at a Japanese car auction. It contains details about the vehicle and its condition at the time of inspection.

Most auction sheets are written in Japanese, which can make them difficult for Pakistani buyers to understand. However, the information can help you identify previous damage, scratches, dents, repairs and other condition-related details.

A typical Japanese auction sheet may include:

  • Vehicle make and model.
  • Chassis or frame number.
  • Vehicle mileage in kilometers.
  • Overall auction grade.
  • Interior and exterior condition grades.
  • Accident and repair information.
  • Damage diagram and inspection marks.
  • Inspector's comments.
  • Auction date and lot number, where available.

Why Is Auction Sheet Verification Important in Pakistan?

Japanese imported cars are popular in Pakistan because of their features, fuel efficiency and different model options. However, buyers do not always have access to the vehicle's original inspection details from Japan.

Some sellers may provide an old, incorrect or edited auction sheet. If the information does not match the actual vehicle, buyers may make a decision based on misleading details.

Here are some reasons why you should verify an auction sheet before purchasing a Japanese imported car:

  • Check original mileage: Compare the recorded auction mileage with the information provided by the seller.
  • Identify previous damage: Review the damage diagram for scratches, dents and repair marks.
  • Understand auction grades: Get a better idea of the vehicle's recorded condition.
  • Check repair history: Look for accident and replacement information recorded by the auction inspector.
  • Make a better decision: Use verified information before negotiating the car's price.

How to Understand Japanese Auction Sheet Grades

Japanese auction sheets use different grades to describe a vehicle's overall condition. Understanding these grades can help buyers compare different cars.

The following table provides a general explanation of commonly used auction grades. Grading standards can vary between auction houses, so always check the original auction house's grading system.

Auction Grade General Meaning
S / 6 Nearly new or exceptionally clean condition.
5 Excellent overall condition with minor wear.
4.5 Very good condition with some minor imperfections.
4 Good used condition with normal signs of use.
3.5 Average condition with noticeable wear or defects.
3 Below-average condition with more visible issues.
2 / 1 Poor condition or significant issues, depending on the auction.
R / RA Generally indicates accident or repair history.

Remember that an auction grade is not a complete mechanical inspection. Always review the full report and arrange an independent physical inspection when possible.

Common Auction Sheet Damage Symbols

Japanese auction sheets use different letters and numbers to identify vehicle damage. These marks are usually shown on a diagram of the car.

Here are some common examples:

  • A1: Small scratch.
  • A2: Medium scratch.
  • A3: Large or noticeable scratch.
  • U1: Small dent.
  • U2: Medium dent.
  • U3: Large dent.
  • W: Surface or repair-related paint irregularity.
  • X: A panel that may require replacement.
  • XX: A panel that has been replaced.
  • S: Rust or corrosion indicator.

These are general examples, and the exact meaning can depend on the auction house and sheet format. Always check the relevant grading legend before interpreting a symbol.

How to Identify a Fake Japanese Auction Sheet

Fake or edited auction sheets can create problems for buyers. A document may look genuine but contain information that does not match the original auction record.

Here are some warning signs to consider:

  • The chassis number does not match the actual vehicle.
  • The mileage is different from the seller's claims.
  • The auction grade looks different from the original record.
  • Damage marks or inspector comments appear to be missing.
  • The vehicle specifications do not match the car.
  • The seller cannot explain where the auction sheet came from.

A screenshot or translated document alone does not prove that an auction sheet is genuine. The best approach is to compare it with the original auction record through a reliable verification process.
